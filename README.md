# Australian -Supply-Chain-Logistics-Performance-Analysis

## Project Overview

This project focuses on analyzing supply chain and logistics operations to evaluate business profitability, transportation costs, shipment performance, warehouse distribution, and operational efficiency.

Using Microsoft Excel, Power Query, Data Modelling, and DAX, I transformed and analyzed logistics data to uncover cost inefficiencies, identify high-cost transportation routes, and provide actionable business recommendations to support better operational and financial decisions.

## Project Objectives

- Evaluate freight revenue against total logistics costs.
- Analyze shipment trends and distribution patterns.
- Identify major transportation cost drivers.
- Evaluate transportation costs across different vehicle types and shipping modes.
- Identify high-cost routes and regional shipment concentrations.
- Assess warehouse shipment distribution.
- Provide recommendations for improving logistics efficiency and profitability.

## Tools & Technologies Used

- **Microsoft Excel:** Data analysis, PivotTables, dashboard development, and visualization.
- **Power Query:** Data cleaning, transformation, and preparation.
- **Data Modelling:** Establishing table relationships for structured analysis.
- **DAX:** Creating calculated measures and KPIs for business performance analysis.

## Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

- Total Freight Revenue
- Total Logistics Cost
- Gross Margin
- Gross Margin Percentage
- Total Shipments
- Average Revenue per Shipment
- Cost per KG
- Cost per KM
- Average Shipment Weight
- Average Delivery Delay

## Key Findings & Insights

### 1. Logistics Costs Exceed Freight Revenue

- Total freight revenue: **$48.34M**
- Total logistics cost: **$53.71M**
- Gross loss: **$5.37M**
- Gross margin: **-11.12%**

Logistics costs exceeded freight revenue, indicating a significant profitability challenge.

### 2. Negative Profitability Across Every Month

Monthly logistics costs consistently exceeded freight revenue throughout the analyzed period. This suggests that the profitability problem is structural rather than being caused by a particular month.

### 3. Relatively Stable Shipment Volume

- Highest monthly shipments: January (1,744).
- Lowest monthly shipments: November (1,577).

Monthly shipment volumes remained relatively stable, suggesting that cost per shipment, route economics, and transportation mode selection deserve closer attention.

### 4. Part-Load Shipments Have the Largest Transportation Cost Share

Part-load shipments account for the largest share of transportation costs, highlighting an opportunity to improve vehicle capacity utilization and shipment consolidation.

### 5. High-Cost Transportation Routes

Two routes stood out for their high cost per KG:

- Melbourne – Perth: $35
- Perth – Sydney: $33

Brisbane – Perth also appeared among the routes requiring further investigation.

### 6. East Coast Has the Highest Regional Shipment Volume

The East Coast recorded approximately 5,040.62 CBM, representing roughly 34% of the displayed regional shipment volume.

This makes the region an important area for operational efficiency improvements.

### 7. High Shipment Concentration at Sydney Distribution Centre

Sydney Distribution Centre handled 13,055 shipments, accounting for approximately 66% of the shipments represented in the warehouse analysis.

This concentration could create congestion risks, handling bottlenecks, and increased dispatch pressure.

The warehouse analysis also reported 19,717 shipments compared with the main KPI of 20,000, leaving a difference of 283 shipments requiring investigation.

### 8. Fuel Is a Major Transportation Cost Driver

- Total transportation cost: $33.97M
- Total fuel cost: $17.16M
- Fuel accounts for approximately 50.5% of transportation costs.

This identifies fuel expenditure as a major opportunity for cost control.

## Business Recommendations

Based on the findings, the following recommendations were proposed:

1. **Investigate Missing Shipments:** Reconcile the 283-shipment difference between the warehouse analysis and the main shipment KPI.

2. **Review Sydney Distribution Centre Capacity:** Investigate warehouse capacity, delivery delays, congestion, and loading/unloading time.

3. **Control Fuel Expenditure:** Monitor vehicle fuel consumption, fuel cost per kilometre, introduce regular vehicle maintenance schedules, and compare vehicle performance across similar routes.

4. **Investigate High-Cost Routes:** Conduct a detailed review of Melbourne–Perth, Perth–Sydney, and Brisbane–Perth to identify opportunities for transportation cost reduction.

5. **Consolidate Part-Load Shipments:** Improve vehicle capacity utilization by consolidating shipments where operationally feasible to reduce transportation costs.

## Conclusion

This project demonstrates how data analytics can support supply chain and logistics decision-making.

The analysis revealed a significant gap between freight revenue and logistics costs, high fuel expenditure, expensive transportation routes, and shipment concentration at Sydney Distribution Centre.

The recommendations focus on improving transportation efficiency, controlling operational costs, investigating data discrepancies, and strengthening overall logistics profitability.
